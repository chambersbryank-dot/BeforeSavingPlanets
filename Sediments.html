Sediments in the field

Grains, structures, and the places that lay sediment down.

Photograph behind the title: NASA, Apollo 17, the Blue Marble, 7 December 1972. Public domain. Not a cartoon globe.

Free self-study outline by Before Saving Planets. It is not the university course it is modeled on, it carries no credit, and that university does not endorse this page. Created by the Founder and SI.

About this course

MIT splits this work into 12.110A Sedimentary Environments and 12.110B Sedimentology in the Field. 12.110A runs from single grains to basins: texture, composition, flow, sedimentary structures, and depositional models. The catalog says 12.110A is not offered in 2027–2028 and lists it for the first half of spring in 2028–2029. 12.110B is a required off-campus trip over spring break, with a measured section and a report. This page is the classroom half. It is not that trip, and MIT does not endorse it. The catalog names L. Nelson for 12.110A.

Modeled on MIT 12.110A and 12.110B. GEOL 310

Size and stuff

Grain size is a clue to the current that moved the sediment. Mud settles where the water is quiet. Sand needs a stronger flow. Composition says what was eroded: quartz lasts, fragments of other rocks tell a nearer source. Do not collect a sample from a river bank for this page.

  1. What does a fine grain suggest about the water?
  2. Why does this course forbid a bank sample?

Structures are the current’s handwriting

Ripples and cross-beds record moving water or wind. Mud cracks record a wet surface that dried. They are not fossils. A depositional model is a picture of a place: river, beach, reef, or deep sea. The rock is the leftover of that place, not a photograph of it.

  1. Name one structure made by moving water and one made by drying.
  2. What is a depositional model?

Read a picture, do not take the rock

The lab is a published photograph or a figure already credited on this site. Write grain size if you can see it, one structure, and a possible place. Say what you cannot see. MIT’s 12.110B adds a measured section on a required trip. That trip is not assigned here.

  1. What three lines does the photo lab ask for?
  2. When does the catalog say 12.110A is not offered?

The field half stays at MIT

12.110B culminates in an oral and written report from data gathered on the trip. Field places vary and may include camping away from services. None of that is this page. If you want the trip, you apply to MIT. L. Nelson is named on their listing, not as a reviewer of Before Saving Planets.

Standards

NGSS text is the official English performance expectation. MS-ESS1-4 says 4.6 billion years. This site uses the USGS age, 4.54 billion years. Keep both numbers visible. C3 text is the College, Career, and Civic Life indicator, in English.

  • 4-ESS1-1 Identify evidence from patterns in rock formations and fossils in rock layers to support an explanation for changes in a landscape over time.
  • MS-ESS1-4 Construct a scientific explanation based on evidence from rock strata for how the geologic time scale is used to organize Earth's 4.6-billion-year-old history.
  • HS-ESS1-5 Evaluate evidence of the past and current movements of continental and oceanic crust and the theory of plate tectonics to explain the ages of crustal rocks.
