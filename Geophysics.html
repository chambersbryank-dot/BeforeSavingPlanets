Geophysics

Waves, gravity, heat, and the inside of a rocky planet.

About this course

MIT 12.002 is Introduction to Geophysics and Planetary Science. The catalog covers terrestrial planets: plates, earthquakes, seismic waves, rheology, impact cratering, gravity and magnetic fields, heat, mantle convection, and the core. It lists Calculus II and Physics II as corequisites. The 2026 subject listing names C. Cattania, M. Pec, and W. Frank. This free outline follows that scope in words. It is not MIT’s class, it skips their problem sets, and MIT does not endorse it.

Modeled on MIT 12.002. GEOL 220

Two body waves

A P wave pushes the rock in the direction it travels. An S wave shears the rock sideways. USGS explains both on its 1906 earthquake waves page. S waves do not pass through liquid. That is why a liquid outer core leaves a shadow for S waves.

  1. Which wave is missing in a liquid?
  2. What page do you cite for P and S waves?

Crust, mantle, core

USGS This Dynamic Earth describes a thin crust, a thick mantle, a liquid outer core, and a solid inner core. Heat leaving the interior helps drive mantle motion. Rheology is how rock flows or breaks: brittle near the surface, slower flow when it is hot and under pressure.

  1. Name the four main shells, from outside in.
  2. What does rheology describe?

Gravity and magnetism

Gravity is stronger where there is more mass. A gravity map is not a topographic map. Earth’s magnetic field is generated in the liquid outer core. It is not a permanent bar magnet frozen in the crust. Compass north is a measurement, and it wanders.

  1. Why is a gravity map not a hill map?
  2. Where does the catalog place the source of the magnetic field?

Craters and siblings

An impact crater is a scar from a collision, not a volcano by default. Chicxulub is the cited crater tied to 66 million years ago on the stratigraphy course. NASA’s solar-system pages are the place to compare Mercury, Venus, Earth, and Mars without inventing a new interior. This page does not calculate an orbit.

  1. What is an impact crater, in one sentence?
  2. Which MIT corequisites does 12.002 list that this page does not teach?

Standards

NGSS text is the official English performance expectation. MS-ESS1-4 says 4.6 billion years. This site uses the USGS age, 4.54 billion years. Keep both numbers visible. C3 text is the College, Career, and Civic Life indicator, in English.

  • 4-ESS1-1 Identify evidence from patterns in rock formations and fossils in rock layers to support an explanation for changes in a landscape over time.
  • MS-ESS1-4 Construct a scientific explanation based on evidence from rock strata for how the geologic time scale is used to organize Earth's 4.6-billion-year-old history.
  • HS-ESS1-5 Evaluate evidence of the past and current movements of continental and oceanic crust and the theory of plate tectonics to explain the ages of crustal rocks.
