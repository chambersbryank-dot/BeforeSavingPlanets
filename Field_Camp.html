Field camp

About this course

MIT 12.115 Field Geology is an intensive two-week off-campus course, usually in the western United States. The catalog lists it for IAP in 2027–2028 and not offered in 2028–2029. Students map bedrock and surficial deposits. The department is the place to ask about a travel fee. The degree chart pairs 12.115 with 12.116, analysis of the data. The 2026 listing names O. Jagoutz and K. Bergmann. This page is a desk camp: read a published map and write the report. It does not buy a ticket, it does not collect rock, and those instructors have not endorsed it.

Modeled on MIT 12.115 Field Geology. GEOL 410

Camp without the van

A real field camp walks the rocks for days. This outline uses the USGS National Geologic Map Database instead. Open one map. Read the legend. Write which colors are bedrock and which are loose surface deposits. Do not drive to the western United States because a paragraph said the MIT course usually goes there.

  1. What database replaces the walk?
  2. Why must you not treat “western United States” as a destination?

Bedrock and loose cover

Bedrock is the solid rock under the loose cover. Surficial deposits are the loose cover: river gravel, dune sand, glacial till. A contact on the map is where the legend changes. Scale and north belong in the note. If the legend does not say it, you do not know it.

  1. What is the difference between bedrock and a surficial deposit?
  2. What do you write when the legend is silent?

Write 12.116 at the desk

The report names the map, the scale, two units, one contact, and what you did not visit. 12.116 on the degree chart is the analysis that follows the camp. Here the analysis is the paragraph. There is no travel fee because there is no travel.

  1. What five things does the desk report name?
  2. What is 12.116 on the degree chart?

Names on a listing

O. Jagoutz and K. Bergmann are named on the 2026 listing for 12.115. That names who teaches MIT’s course when it runs. It does not make them authors of this page. The catalog also says to contact the department about funding for the travel fee. This site cannot answer that question.

  1. What does a name on the MIT listing mean here?
  2. Who answers a question about MIT’s travel fee?

Standards

NGSS text is the official English performance expectation. MS-ESS1-4 says 4.6 billion years. This site uses the USGS age, 4.54 billion years. Keep both numbers visible. C3 text is the College, Career, and Civic Life indicator, in English.

  • 4-ESS1-1 Identify evidence from patterns in rock formations and fossils in rock layers to support an explanation for changes in a landscape over time.
  • MS-ESS1-4 Construct a scientific explanation based on evidence from rock strata for how the geologic time scale is used to organize Earth's 4.6-billion-year-old history.
  • HS-ESS1-5 Evaluate evidence of the past and current movements of continental and oceanic crust and the theory of plate tectonics to explain the ages of crustal rocks.
