A giant shark

Otodus megalodon

Teeth, not an invented length.

Drawing: Oliver E. Demuth, CC BY 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons. Artist’s concept, not a photograph. Drawing file page

There is no film of this animal made for this site. The picture behind the title is a drawing. The photograph lower down is fossil teeth.

What we know

  • Boessenecker and others, 2019, record Otodus megalodon from middle Miocene rocks through the early Pliocene, and put the extinction at about 3.6 million years ago. PeerJ paper
  • The teeth in the photograph are from the Capistrano Formation. That paper’s corrected age for this set is 5.6 to 3.7 million years. PeerJ article page
  • The drawing behind the title is the artist’s restoration. This page does not copy a body length from the drawing, and it does not draw a person beside the shark.
  • The pin is San Juan Capistrano, the town the formation is named for. It is not one hole in the rock. Created by the Founder and SI.

Sources (4)

Where the teeth are

Not one hole

The pin is the town of San Juan Capistrano, California. The formation is named for that town. It is not one hole marked to the meter.
