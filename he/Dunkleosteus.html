An armored fish

Dunkleosteus

A bony helmet, blades instead of teeth, and a shorter body than the old museum monsters.

Fossil: Russell K. Engelman, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons. The skull and the belly armor are real. Some side plates are rebuilt. Fossil file page

What we know

  • Before animals with legs were common, the seas had armored hunters. Dunkleosteus, from the shale under Cleveland, wore a bony helmet and had self-sharpening jaw blades instead of teeth. The age used on this site’s Cleveland Shale coin is about 360 million years ago, near the end of the Devonian.
  • The photograph is skull CMNH 5768 at the Cleveland Museum of Natural History. The skull and the belly armor are real. Some of the side plates are rebuilt. Fossil file page Engelman, 2024
  • Engelman, 2024, gives about 3.0 to 3.4 metres for subadults to typical adults, and about 4.1 metres for the largest individuals, from the method in his 2023 study. The same paper says lengths a little higher, about 3.8 and 4.5 metres, remain possible, and that the old pictures of an 8 to 10 metre fish are not supported. The body behind the armor is poorly known, so these are estimates, not a tape measure.
  • The small drawing is an artist’s concept of that shorter fish. It is not a photograph. The file is 634 pixels wide, and this page does not enlarge it. Drawing file page

What this page does not claim

  • A typical adult estimate of about 3 to 3.4 metres is longer than a person. This page does not draw a person beside the fish, because the length is an estimate and the tail is poorly known.
  • The pin is Doan Brook in Cleveland, the brook the Cleveland Shale was named for. Armor has been found in that shale under the city and in nearby counties, not in one single hole. The marker was made for this site. Created by the Founder and SI.

Where the fossil is

Not one hole

The pin is Doan Brook in Cleveland. The armor comes from the shale under the city and from nearby counties.
