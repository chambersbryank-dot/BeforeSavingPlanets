A lost bird of prey

Haast’s eagle

A skull plate, not an invented wingspan.

Drawing: John Megahan, CC BY 2.5, via Wikimedia Commons. An illustration, not a photograph. Drawing file page

TODO machine-draft translation: English for review.

There is no film of this animal made for this site. The picture behind the title is a drawing. The figure lower down is a skull plate, not a life portrait.

What we know

  • New Zealand Birds Online says Haast’s eagle died out 500 to 600 years ago, around the same time as the moa. New Zealand Birds Online
  • The figure below is a skull plate from Castle Rocks, Southland. It is not a life portrait. This page does not give a wingspan, because it does not invent one.
  • The drawing behind the title shows the eagle and a moa. It is an artist’s illustration, not a photograph of either animal.
  • The pin is the Southland region, the region named with that skull plate. It is not one hole. Created by the Founder and SI.

Sources (3)

  • New Zealand Birds Online: Haast’s eagle
  • Wikimedia Commons — the Castle Rocks skull plate, public domain
  • Wikimedia Commons — the illustration, CC BY 2.5

Where the plate is from

The region, not one hole

The plate is from Castle Rocks, Southland. The pin marks the Southland region, not one site to the meter.
