مواقع الأحافير حول العالم
  • Burgess Shale, British Columbia, Canada — علامة مؤقتة — صفحة موثّقة قريباً
  • Messel Pit, Germany — علامة مؤقتة — صفحة موثّقة قريباً
  • Dinosaur Provincial Park, Alberta, Canada — علامة مؤقتة — صفحة موثّقة قريباً
  • La Brea Tar Pits, Los Angeles, USA — علامة مؤقتة — صفحة موثّقة قريباً
  • Ediacara Hills, South Australia — علامة مؤقتة — صفحة موثّقة قريباً
  • Wadi Al-Hitan, Egypt — علامة مؤقتة — صفحة موثّقة قريباً
  • Ischigualasto, Argentina — علامة مؤقتة — صفحة موثّقة قريباً
  • Monte San Giorgio, Switzerland/Italy — علامة مؤقتة — صفحة موثّقة قريباً
  • Mistaken Point, Newfoundland, Canada — 580–560 Ma. Some of the oldest large, complex animal-like life ever found. These are soft, frond-shaped seafloor creatures pressed into rock. UNESCO World Heritage: Mistaken Point
  • Chengjiang Fossil Site, Yunnan, China — ~530 Ma. Animals preserved in amazing detail, sometimes including soft body parts. The pictures are Fuxianhuia protensa, an arthropod from this site. UNESCO World Heritage: Chengjiang Fossil Site
  • Joggins Fossil Cliffs, Nova Scotia, Canada — Carboniferous. Whole fossil trees still stand upright in the cliffs, and some of the earliest reptiles were preserved inside hollow trunks. UNESCO World Heritage: Joggins Fossil Cliffs Parks Canada: Joggins Fossil Cliffs
  • Petrified Forest National Park, Arizona, USA — Late Triassic. Logs that slowly turned into colorful stone (quartz), along with early dinosaurs and their relatives. The drawing is Chindesaurus, an early dinosaur found in this park. National Park Service: petrified wood National Park Service: dinosaurs of Petrified Forest
  • Dinosaur National Monument, Utah/Colorado, USA — Jurassic. A rock wall in the Quarry Exhibit Hall still holds about 1,500 dinosaur bones. The National Park Service names Allosaurus, Apatosaurus, Camarasaurus, Diplodocus, and Stegosaurus among them. National Park Service: Quarry Exhibit Hall
  • Jurassic Coast (Dorset & East Devon), UK — ~185 Myr record. 155 km of coastline whose rock layers hold about 185 million years of Earth history, including marine reptiles such as Ichthyosaurus from Lyme Regis. UNESCO World Heritage: Dorset and East Devon Coast
  • Riversleigh & Naracoorte, Australia — Cenozoic. Fossils of ancient marsupials. The pictures are Nimbadon lavarackorum, a wombat relative from Riversleigh. Naracoorte’s caves also hold ice-age animals, including the marsupial lion. UNESCO World Heritage: Australian Fossil Mammal Sites
  • Cradle of Humankind, South Africa — Plio-Pleistocene. Caves full of fossils of early human relatives, including the Taung Child, a young Australopithecus africanus. UNESCO World Heritage: Fossil Hominid Sites of South Africa
