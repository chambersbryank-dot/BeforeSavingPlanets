The journey

The long story sits on this shelf: a molten world, the first oceans, the measured maps, and why this library exists.

Created by the Founder and SI

4.6 billion years ago

A molten world

Earth opens as cracked lava. No oceans yet, and no life — a planet of fire. This is the gasp. The slow road after it is why Before Saving Planets exists: so the journey can be understood, not skipped.

Created by the Founder and SI

Older than 540 million years

Crust, then water

The crust cools and the first oceans pool in the cracks. These continents are not ours yet. This stretch is an artist’s interpretation, labeled as such — the measured maps begin at 540 million years. The oldest Dig Quest layers live here.

Scotese PALEOMAP via GPlates (EarthByte)

540 million years ago

The maps take over

From here the land is a PALEOMAP reconstruction. The camera stays where Africa is today. The continents drift into that view. They are not dissolved into each other.

Scotese PALEOMAP via GPlates (EarthByte)

540 million years → today

The long drift

Plates slide. Supercontinents gather and break apart. An asteroid strikes. Ice advances and retreats. Every Dig Quest layer is a stop on this ride.

National Park Service, public domain

The reason for this site

Why Before Saving Planets exists

The fossil record is a field guide for the planet we still have. Dig into the evidence, move the globe, then cross to Saving Planets for the work of healing — oceans, forests, reefs, and poles.
